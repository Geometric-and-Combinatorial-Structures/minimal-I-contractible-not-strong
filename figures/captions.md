# Figure captions (manuscript: *Minimal graphs that are I-contractible but not strong I-contractible*)

All vertex labels are the graph6 labels 0..n−1 of the g6 strings quoted below, except in Fig. 2a (dunce hat), which uses the labels 1..15 of DEFH23 Fig. 2A. Unfolded discs are drawn with the algorithm of the text: cut the clique complex along every edge contained in ≠ 2 triangles, glue corners of adjacent triangles across the edges in exactly 2 triangles, and embed the resulting disc with Tutte's barycentric embedding, the boundary on a regular polygon. Boundary vertices are red and carry their original label; the boundary word is read clockwise from the top vertex. **Figure 1. The smallest flag complex with odd torsion: G₁₃ = `L?qa``{wttym~o`.**
(a) The clique complex Δ(G₁₃) (13 vertices, 40 edges, 28 triangles, K₄-free) cut along the four edges of the 4-cycle 8–10–9–11 (the only edges in 3 triangles) unfolds into a triangulated disc with 21 vertices whose 12-gon boundary reads (10 9 11 8)³: the boundary runs three times, in the same direction, around the cycle *a* = 8–10–9–11 (arcs). (b) Hence Δ(G₁₃) is the Moore space M(ℤ/3, 1): a disc whose boundary wraps three times around a circle; H₁(Δ; ℤ) = ℤ/3, H₂ = 0, so Δ is ℤ₂-acyclic but not contractible. (c) The graph G₁₃ itself, drawn with vertex 12 (degree 8) at the centre, its neighbourhood cycle C₈ around it and the 4-cycle *a* outside (red). G₁₃ is the unique stuck graph on 13 vertices (Theorem A); its two I₄-closures `L?qa`p{wttym~o` (41 edges) and `L?qahp{wttym~o` (42 edges) have the same homology.

**Figure 6. Strong I-contractibility certificate of G14a + {0,1} = `M_b@bFHNEk@~|k}U?` (Theorem D).**
Starting from G14a with the I₄ edge {0,1} glued (blue, dashed), the 18 deletions listed in `traza_G14_mas01.txt` reduce the graph to a single vertex: steps 1–5 delete edges {u,v} whose common neighbourhood N(u,v) is a cone or K₁ (I₂ moves), steps 6–18 delete vertices whose neighbourhood N(v) is 0-dismantlable (I₁ moves). The header states what does and does not change: both graphs are contractible, only strong I-contractibility differs. In each panel the deleted element is red and its (common) neighbourhood amber (vertices filled, induced edges as a halo); the vertex positions are those of Fig. 3b. Every step was re-verified when drawing (the neighbourhood printed in the title is recomputed from the current graph). The same graph without the edge {0,1} is not strong I-contractible, so {0,1} is an I_S-contractible edge whose removal leaves 𝔍_S — the minimal instance of the phenomenon of DEFH23 Conjecture 15.

**Figure 8. The 2-connectivity lemma (Lemma 3.2).**
If a stuck graph G has a cut vertex u, write G = A ∪_u B. For every w ∈ A − u the whole neighbourhood N(w) lies in A, so N(w) = N_A(w) is not acyclic, and every edge of A keeps its common neighbourhood: A and B are *almost stuck* (ℤ₂-acyclic, every vertex other than u with non-acyclic neighbourhood, every edge stuck). The computation finds no almost-stuck piece on ≤ 11 vertices, hence every stuck graph on ≤ 22 vertices is 2-connected; consequently it has no bridge, every edge lies in ≥ 2 triangles, the minimum degree is ≥ 4 and the maximum degree is ≤ n − 2, which justifies the generator options `-C -d4 -D(n−2)`.

**Figure 9. The distributed computation on YUCA (ACARUS, Universidad de Sonora).**
Graphs are generated by `geng` in res/mod slices and each slice is processed by one CPU running Algorithm 1, a cascade of increasingly expensive tests (no neighbourhood is a cone → χ(Δ) = 1 → no N(v) ℤ₂-acyclic → no N(u,v) ℤ₂-acyclic → full ℤ₂-homology of Δ); survivors are the stuck graphs. For n = 13 the cascade reduces 2.10 × 10¹³ connected graphs with 4 ≤ deg ≤ 11 to 3.34 × 10¹², 4.80 × 10¹¹, 1.57 × 10¹⁰, 218,516 and finally 1 stuck graph. Post-processing: closure under I₄ gluings (all ℤ₂-acyclic non-𝔍_S graphs of that order), integer homology by Smith normal form, and certificates (I₄ gluing plus I_S deletion trace). Runs: n = 12, 1.64 × 10¹¹ graphs, 8 min on 448 CPUs (row 12 of the table); n = 13, 2.10 × 10¹³ graphs, 7 h 10 min on 448 CPUs, 200,000 slices; n = 14 K₄-free, 6.49 × 10¹³ graphs, ≈ 33 h on 896 CPUs under two SLURM accounts, 2,000,000 slices.
