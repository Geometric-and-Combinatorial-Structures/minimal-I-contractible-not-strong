\section{The extended cardinality table}\label{sec:table}

Table~\ref{tab:card} reports the numbers of connected graphs in the classes of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. The seven shared columns in rows $3\le n\le11$ agree with Table~1 of~\cite{DEFH2023}, which already includes $\I$. For $n\le12$, the reported equality of the acyclic and strong columns gives the $\I$ column by the inclusions $\IS\subseteq\I\subseteq\{\Ztwo\text{-acyclic graphs}\}$; no separate search for arbitrary I-transformations is required. The order-$12$ row combines two runs: the first computed six columns, including $|\IVS(12)|=\num{22666525713}$; the second, with the final program, computed all columns and reproduced those six exactly. The order-$13$ row enumerates all $\num{50335907869219}$ connected graphs on $13$ vertices (OEIS A001349) in $\num{200000}$ slices (Appendix~\ref{app:cluster}). Without the reductions of Section~\ref{sec:reduction}, it finds exactly three $\Ztwo$-acyclic graphs outside $\IS$ without a certificate of at most three (I4) additions, and these are the three torsion graphs of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}; hence $|\I(13)|=|\IS(13)|$, and the row agrees with Theorem~\ref{thm:A} by an independent route. Hypothesis $\mathrm{H12}$ and the implementation qualification in Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} apply to the strong-membership entries of rows $12$ and $13$.

Several patterns in the table are worth recording.

\begin{observation}\label{obs:table}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item Under $\mathrm{H12}$, for $n\le12$ every $\Ztwo$-acyclic graph is strong I-contractible: $\Ztwo\text{-acyclic}=\I=\IS$; in particular $\Ztwo$- and $\ZZ$-acyclicity coincide for $n\le12$. The first exceptions occur at $n=13$ and are the three torsion graphs of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}; the first members of $\I\setminus\IS$ occur at $n=14$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:B}).
\item $\IVS=2\text{-Dis}=3\text{-Dis}$ for all $n\le13$, so a graph in $\IVS$ that is not $2$-dismantlable has at least $14$ vertices; such graphs are known to exist~\cite[Proposition~8]{FieuxJouve2020}. At $n=12$ this agrees with an independent (unpublished, 2025) computation of $|2\text{-Dis}(12)|$ by the authors. On the other hand $1\text{-Dis}=2\text{-Dis}$ for $n\le11$, while $|2\text{-Dis}(12)|-|1\text{-Dis}(12)|=20$ and $|2\text{-Dis}(13)|-|1\text{-Dis}(13)|=\num{35757}$; the twenty graphs of order $12$ are listed in the repository (\protect\path{data/strictly_2dis_12.g6}, with $2$-dismantling sequences in \protect\path{certificates/strictly_2dis_12_traces.txt}). The $\IVS$ and $k$-Dis columns are decided by exact dynamic programming over vertex subsets and do not depend on the memoisation discussed in Section~\ref{sec:algorithms}.
\item $\IS\ne\IVS$ first at $n=11$ (twelve graphs; the smallest members of $\IS\setminus\IVS$ of~\cite{DEFH2023}); the reported differences are $|\IS(12)|-|\IVS(12)|=\num{11718}$ and $|\IS(13)|-|\IVS(13)|=\num{14948703}$.
\item $\chi=1$ but not $\Ztwo$-acyclic first at $n=7$ (one graph), and the gap grows to $\num{9816037137}$ graphs at $n=12$ and $\num{4240933193936}$ at $n=13$. The column $(-1)$-Dis counts cones, that is, all graphs on $n-1$ vertices (OEIS A000088: $\num{1018997864}$ for $n=12$ and $\num{165091172592}$ for $n=13$), which provides a consistency check.
\end{enumerate}
\end{observation}
