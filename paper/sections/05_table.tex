\section{The extended cardinality table}\label{sec:table}

Table~\ref{tab:card} lists, for connected graphs on $n\le 13$ vertices, the number of graphs in each of the classes of Section~\ref{sec:prelim}. The rows $n\le 11$ coincide cell by cell with Table~1 of~\cite{DEFH2023}; the column $\I$ is new (it was obtained by certifying every $\Ztwo$-acyclic graph outside $\IS$ with Algorithm~\ref{alg:icert}, which for $n\le 12$ is vacuous since every $\Ztwo$-acyclic graph is in $\IS$). The row $n=12$ was computed on the cluster in $8$ minutes (Table~\ref{tab:runs}) and gives the new value $|\IVS(12)|=\num{22666525713}$; the columns $\I$, $3$-Dis, $2$-Dis, $1$-Dis and $(-1)$-Dis for $n=12$ come from a second run with the final version of the program (also $8$ minutes), which reproduced the six columns of the first run exactly. The row $n=13$ (all $\num{50335907869219}$ connected graphs, $\num{200000}$ slices, about $21$ hours on $896$ cores) is new in its entirety and provides an independent check of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}: without any of the reductions of Section~\ref{sec:reduction}, the program found exactly three $\Ztwo$-acyclic graphs that are not strong I-contractible and admit no certificate of I-contractibility by at most three (I4) additions, and they are precisely the three torsion graphs (\texttt{yuca/logs/n13\_tabla\_indecisas.txt}); hence $|\I(13)|=|\IS(13)|=\num{5619022190283}$ and $|\Ztwo\text{-acyclic}(13)|-|\IS(13)|=3$.

Several patterns in the table are worth recording.

\begin{observation}\label{obs:table}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item For $n\le 12$ every $\Ztwo$-acyclic graph is strong I-contractible: $\Ztwo\text{-acyclic}=\I=\IS$. The first exceptions occur at $n=13$ and are the three torsion graphs of Theorem~\ref{thm:A}; in particular ``$\Ztwo$-acyclic'' and ``$\ZZ$-acyclic'' coincide for $n\le 12$. The first $\ZZ$-acyclic graphs outside $\IS$, and the first members of $\I\setminus\IS$, occur at $n=14$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:B}).
\item $\IVS=2\text{-Dis}=3\text{-Dis}$ for all $n\le 13$ (for $n=12$, $|\IVS(12)|$ agrees with the value of $|2\text{-Dis}(12)|$ obtained in an independent, unpublished 2025 computation by the authors); a graph in $\IVS$ that is not $2$-dismantlable, if any, has at least $14$ vertices. On the other hand $1\text{-Dis}=2\text{-Dis}$ for $n\le 11$ while $|2\text{-Dis}(12)|-|1\text{-Dis}(12)|=20$ and $|2\text{-Dis}(13)|-|1\text{-Dis}(13)|=\num{35757}$: the smallest graphs that are $2$-dismantlable but not $1$-dismantlable have $12$ vertices, and there are exactly $20$ of them.
\item $\IS\ne\IVS$ first at $n=11$ (twelve graphs; the smallest members of $\IS\setminus\IVS$ of~\cite{DEFH2023}), $|\IS(12)|-|\IVS(12)|=\num{11718}$ and $|\IS(13)|-|\IVS(13)|=\num{14948703}$.
\item $\chi=1$ but not $\Ztwo$-acyclic first at $n=7$ (one graph), and the gap grows to $\num{9816037137}$ graphs at $n=12$ and $\num{4240933193936}$ at $n=13$. The column $(-1)$-Dis counts cones, i.e.\ all graphs on $n-1$ vertices ($\num{1018997864}$ for $n=12$ and $\num{165091172592}$ for $n=13$, OEIS A000088), a useful consistency check.
\end{enumerate}
\end{observation}
